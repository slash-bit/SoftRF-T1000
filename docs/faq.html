Troubleshooting / FAQ

Answers to the questions that come up most often in the Telegram group. For normal use of the device, see the T1000E Card User Guide.

Installing & Updating

The device is frozen, the LED is pulsing or solid, and it won't switch off

First, reset it: hold the button and attach the magnetic connector while the cable is plugged into a powered USB port.

If a drive named T1000-E appears on your computer, the card is stuck in update (DFU) mode. Copy the firmware .uf2 file onto that drive again and let the update finish.

Note A card in DFU mode can't be switched off with the button. If you can't get it out of DFU mode, letting the battery run flat will also reset it, but this can take about 4 days.

Windows says "file couldn't be copied", "device disappeared" or error 0x80070022

This is normal. The card installs the firmware and restarts so quickly that Windows loses the drive before it finishes the copy. The update has worked; press the button to start the device.

The one real problem to look out for is a broken download. If the firmware .uf2 file is much smaller than about 900 KB (for example around 220 KB), or still has a .crdownload extension, the download was cut short. Download it again. The same applies to the bootloader flasher .exe, which should be about 15 MB.

The LED is flickering after I copied the firmware. Should I copy it again?

No. The flickering LED means the firmware is still being written. Wait until the card switches itself off (up to about 30 seconds), then press the button to start it. Copying the file again or unplugging the card during this time can leave it stuck in update mode (see above).

How do I check which firmware is installed?

Open the Status page of the Configurator. It shows the running firmware version and tells you if a newer one is available.

Don't rely on sw_version in settings.json: it is only updated when settings are saved, so after an update it can still show the old version.

Can I set up a brand-new card from a Mac?

Yes. The online Bootloader Flasher runs in the browser and works on a Mac as well as on Windows, so you don't need the Windows .exe flasher. After flashing the bootloader, continue with the SoftDevice and firmware steps in Getting Started.

Tracking

Why can't I see myself on Puretrack or OGN?

Check these in order:

  1. Is a ground station in range? The card transmits at low power, and the signal needs a clear line of sight to an OGN or PilotAware ground station. On the ground, hills, buildings and your own body often block it. In the air it usually works without problems. Check the nearest stations on the ground station map.
  2. Is your ID registered? Register the ID shown in the Configurator on OGN DDB (device type FLARM) and on Puretrack. A new registration can take until the next day to appear. If you upgraded from v007, your ID has probably changed to 88xxxx — see Your aircraft ID.
  3. Does Puretrack hide you? Turn on Show Unidentified labels in Puretrack, or link your ID to your Puretrack account.
  4. Flying FANET only? FANET range to ground stations is often shorter than FLARM. Another pilot's Skytraxx or Oudie can also forward your FANET position to OGN.

You don't need to be airborne to be tracked: the card transmits from power-on. The airborne state only controls the alarms and flight logging.

To see after a flight where and how far you were received:

  • OGN range analyser — all protocols, UK only
  • PilotAware Vector — FLARM only
  • KTrax — FLARM only
